01 / THE IDEA

WHEN A MISSION ENDS...

What happens to a machine when its mission is over?

Not every space explorer returns to Earth. Some machines remain on distant worlds, resting where their missions ended.

From the Moon to Mars, NASA's spacecraft and rovers have helped scientists explore places humans have never walked. Even after their missions ended, the evidence they collected continues to shape our understanding of the Solar System.

These are not just forgotten machines. They are part of humanity's story of discovery.

EARTH'S NATURAL SATELLITE

THE MOON

THE MOON · 1972

LUNAR ROVING VEHICLE

Apollo 17
Moon Buggy

A small electric vehicle that helped astronauts travel farther across the Moon than they could on foot.

01 — MEET THE EXPLORER

The Lunar Roving Vehicle was used during Apollo 17 in December 1972. Astronauts Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt drove it across the lunar surface.

02 — WHY WAS IT SENT?

Walking on the Moon limited how far astronauts could explore. The rover gave them a way to travel farther, carry equipment, and collect more lunar samples.

03 — ITS GREATEST CONTRIBUTION

It helped the astronauts collect and study lunar rocks and soil from a wider area, including samples that improved scientists' understanding of the Moon's geological history.

04 — WHERE IS IT NOW?

The Apollo 17 rover remains on the Moon at the Taurus-Littrow valley. It was left there when the astronauts returned to Earth.

02

THE RED PLANET

MARS

MARS · 1997

MARS PATHFINDER ROVER

Sojourner

Small in size, but a giant step in the history of exploring Mars.

01 — MEET THE EXPLORER

Sojourner was the first successful rover to operate on Mars. It arrived with the Mars Pathfinder mission in July 1997.

02 — WHY WAS IT SENT?

NASA sent Sojourner to test whether a small robotic rover could move across Mars, study nearby rocks, and send useful information back to Earth.

03 — ITS GREATEST DISCOVERY

Sojourner examined Martian rocks and soil, helping scientists study the planet's composition and understand its geological past. It also proved that rover exploration on Mars was possible.

04 — WHERE IS IT NOW?

Sojourner remains at the Mars Pathfinder landing site in Ares Vallis. Its mission ended after communication with the lander was lost.

MARS · 2004

MARS EXPLORATION ROVER

Spirit

A determined explorer that kept working long after its expected mission.

01 — MEET THE EXPLORER

Spirit was one of NASA's twin Mars Exploration Rovers. It landed in Gusev Crater in January 2004.

02 — WHY WAS IT SENT?

Spirit was designed to search for signs that water once changed the rocks and environment of Mars.

03 — ITS GREATEST DISCOVERY

Spirit found evidence of ancient water-related activity, including rocks and soil altered by water. These findings helped scientists understand that Mars once had environments very different from today's dry surface.

04 — WHERE IS IT NOW?

Spirit remains on Mars in the Gusev Crater region, near a location known as Troy. NASA ended the mission in 2011 after attempts to restore communication failed.

MARS · 2004

MARS EXPLORATION ROVER

Opportunity

A rover that turned a short mission into years of exploration.

01 — MEET THE EXPLORER

Opportunity was Spirit's twin rover. It landed on Mars in January 2004, in a region called Meridiani Planum.

02 — WHY WAS IT SENT?

Its mission was to study Martian rocks and soil for evidence that liquid water had once existed on the planet.

03 — ITS GREATEST DISCOVERY

Opportunity found strong evidence that liquid water once existed on ancient Mars. It also explored craters and traveled much farther than originally planned.

04 — WHERE IS IT NOW?

Opportunity remains in Perseverance Valley, on the rim of Endeavour Crater. A planet-wide dust storm blocked sunlight from its solar panels, and NASA ended the mission in 2019 after communication could not be restored.
